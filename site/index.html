Three steps, six parts. Solid border = I trained or wrote it. Dashed border = pretrained, used as-is.

InputPhotoJPG, PNG or WebP

I did not train any large model from scratch. Training a captioner or a language model needs millions of examples and many GPUs. Reusing them is called transfer learning.

04

Mood classifier: 5 models compared

Same features, same pipeline, same folds. KNN wins on cross-validation with 87.5 %; Logistic Regression is almost tied at 87.0 %.

CLIP vector512 numbers→ StandardScalermean 0, std 1→ PCA90 % variance → 98→ one of 5 classifiersbelow→ mood+ probabilities

Scaler and PCA sit inside one scikit-learn Pipeline, so in every fold they are fitted on the training part only. No data leakage. I choose by CV mean (5 scores) rather than test accuracy (one small set of 51 photos). The final model is refit on all 251 photos and saved for the app.

05

Without labels: do moods group by themselves?

Only weakly. K-Means with k = 4 matches my moods at an ARI of 0.28 (0 = chance, 1 = perfect) and the clusters overlap (silhouette 0.05). CLIP groups photos mostly by content, so labels are needed.

06

Poem model: building data and fine-tuning

No image-to-poem dataset existed, so I built one, then trained a LoRA adapter on it in 6 minutes on my laptop's GPU.

  1. Caption + moodBLIP caption and the true mood label for each photo.
  2. Split by photo80 / 10 / 10. Test photos never get poems.
  3. 8 candidatesBase Qwen with a long, strict “teacher” prompt writes 8 poems per photo.
  4. Keep the bestMy scorer rates them; keep 8-line poems scoring ≥ 0.45, up to 2 per photo.
  5. Short promptTrain on the short everyday prompt, so the model learns the rules by itself.
Write a short 8-line poem about: {caption}. Mood: {mood}. Use vivid imagery, simple words, and a gentle rhyme.
Training settings (from train_info.json)

Loss = cross-entropy on poem tokens only (prompt tokens get label −100). The adapter is saved at the last step (102), after the best validation step (30).

Limits

  • Small, one labeller. 251 photos labelled by me alone; ± 5 points between folds.
  • Only four moods, and they overlap (a sunny beach is happy and calm).
  • Overconfident. KNN often says 100 %: it is a vote share, not a calibrated probability.
  • Captions can be wrong; the poem follows the mistake.
  • Poems rarely rhyme (only ~8 % of targets did).
  • My scorer counts words, it cannot judge beauty.
  • Adapter saved at the last step, after validation loss started rising.

Next steps

  1. Rebuild poem data keeping only rhyming candidates, retrain.
  2. Keep the best validation checkpoint (early stopping).
  3. More photos, labelled by two classmates too; measure agreement.
  4. Calibrate the mood probabilities.
  5. Blind human rating of base vs fine-tuned poems.
Accuracy
The share of predictions that are correct.
ARI
Adjusted Rand Index. Agreement between two groupings (here clusters and moods), corrected for chance: 0 = random, 1 = identical.
Batch
The group of examples used for one weight update.
Confusion matrix
A table of true class (rows) against predicted class (columns). The diagonal holds the correct answers.
Cross-validation
Split the training data into k parts, train on k-1 and test on the one left out, repeat k times and average. A fairer score than one split.
Data leakage
When information from test data sneaks into training (for example fitting PCA on all images), so the score looks better than it really is.
Embedding
A list of numbers that describes something, here a photo. Images that look or mean alike get similar numbers. CLIP gives each image 512 numbers.
Epoch
One full pass over the training data.
Fine-tuning
Continuing to train a pretrained model on my own small dataset so it adapts to my task.
Gradient accumulation
Add up gradients from several small batches and update once. Acts like a bigger batch with less memory.
K-Means
Clustering that splits data into k groups by repeatedly assigning points to the nearest centre and moving each centre to the mean of its points.
k-nearest neighbours
Labels a new point by a vote of the k most similar training points.
LoRA
Low-Rank Adaptation. Freeze the big weight matrices and learn two small matrices whose product is added to them. Cheap fine-tuning.
Loss
The number training tries to make small. For text it is cross-entropy: how surprised the model is by the correct next token.
Overfitting
The model memorises the training data and gets worse on new data. Visible as training loss falling while validation loss rises.
PCA
Principal Component Analysis. Rotates the data so the first new axes hold most of the variation; the rest can be dropped.
Precision, recall, F1
Precision: of the images I called sad, how many were sad. Recall: of the sad images, how many I found. F1: the harmonic mean of the two.
Pretrained / frozen
A model whose weights someone else already learned on a large dataset. Frozen means I use those weights as they are and never change them.
Silhouette score
Cluster quality from -1 to 1: how much closer each point is to its own cluster than to the next one. Near 0 means the clusters overlap.
Standardisation
Shifting and stretching each feature to mean 0 and standard deviation 1, so no single feature dominates distances.
Stratified split
A split that keeps the same share of each class in every part.
t-SNE
A method that places points on a 2-D map so close neighbours stay close. Good for looking, not for measuring distances.
Temperature and top-p
How the model picks the next token. Temperature below 1 makes choices safer, above 1 more random. Top-p keeps only the most likely tokens that together make up p of the probability.
Token
The pieces a language model reads and writes: whole words or parts of words.
Transfer learning
Reusing what a model learned on a big task as the starting point for a small one. I reuse CLIP's image features instead of learning vision from 251 photos.
